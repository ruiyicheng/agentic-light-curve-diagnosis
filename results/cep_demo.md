 # Light Curve Analysis Diagnosis

## Primary Classification: **Classical Cepheid**

| Variability Class | Probability | Reasoning |
|------------------|-------------|-----------|
| **Cepheid** | **Very High** | Period P ≈ 3.88 days falls squarely in the Classical Cepheid range (3–50 days). The amplitude of ~0.6 mag (13.5 → 14.1) is characteristic of Cepheids. Crucially, the phase-folded curve at P (top-right) displays the classic **"shark-fin" morphology**: a slow, gradual decline (~2.5 phase units) followed by a rapid, steep rise (~1.5 phase units). The asymmetric pulse shape is confirmed by the **P/2 panel (bottom-left)**, which shows characteristic **bifurcation** (two distinct tracks) - this occurs because folding an asymmetric light curve at half its period superimposes phases with different magnitudes (φ and φ+0.5), creating a double-valued sequence. The 2P panel shows clean repetition of two cycles, confirming P is the fundamental period. |
| **RRab** | Very Low | RR Lyrae stars have periods of 0.3–1.2 days. The observed period of 3.88 days is far too long for this class. Additionally, while RRab show similar asymmetric morphology, the P/2 bifurcation test here combined with the >3 day period excludes RR Lyrae types. |
| **RRc** | Very Low | RRc stars have even shorter periods (0.2–0.5 days) and sinusoidal, low-amplitude (~0.2–0.4 mag) light curves. The 3.88-day period and 0.6-mag asymmetric amplitude are incompatible. |
| **RRd** | Very Low | Double-mode RR Lyrae pulsate simultaneously in two periods (fundamental P₀ ≈ 0.5 d and overtone P₁ ≈ 0.35 d). The 3.88-day period does not match RRd physics, and the light curve shows no evidence of beating between two close periods. |
| **EW (W UMa)** | Very Low | Contact binaries have continuous light variation but periods of only 0.25–0.75 days. A 3.88-day period is physically impossible for W UMa systems (Roche lobe overflow requires much shorter orbital periods). |
| **EA (Algol)** | Low | While EA binaries can have P ≈ 4 days, they exhibit **flat sections** outside of eclipse with two distinct, narrow minima. The top-right panel shows **smooth, continuous variation** with no flat regions, ruling out detached eclipsing binaries. |
| **Delta Scuti** | Very Low | δ Scuti stars have periods of 0.05–0.3 days and amplitudes typically <0.1 mag (rarely up to 0.3 mag). The 3.88-day period is an order of magnitude too long, and the 0.6-mag amplitude is too high. |

## Diagnostic Key: The P/2 Bifurcation Test
The bottom-left panel (P/2 = 1.94 d) is the decisive diagnostic. The scattered, double-track appearance occurs because:
- Phase φ and φ+0.5 of the original period map to the same phase in P/2
- For an **asymmetric** Cepheid curve: mag(φ) ≠ mag(φ+0.5)
- This creates two parallel sequences in the folded diagram
- A **sinusoidal** curve (δ Sct, ellipsoidal) would collapse to a single track at P/2
- An **eclipsing binary** folded at wrong period would show incoherent scatter, not organized bifurcation

This confirms the **asymmetric pulsating variable** classification and validates P = 3.878 d as the true period rather than an alias.
